Implemented an independent x264 race experiment. The selector uses an 8-configuration random warm start, then fits a RandomForestRegressor and evaluates four batches of eight predicted-best candidates (32 evaluations total). The baseline uniformly samples 32 candidates. Each method uses the same 20 seeds and 75/25 shuffled candidate split; quality is regret against the best performance in the complete validated dataset (lower is better). The selector won on mean regret (1.253% vs 2.392%) and mean best performance (247.290 vs 250.072), at the expected cost of model-fitting runtime. The advantage is an average, not a guarantee for every seed: variability was 2.257% versus 2.622% regret standard deviation. Results assume lower Performance- is better, rows are independent measured configurations, and the dataset is representative. Limits include one dataset, one fixed budget, no measurement noise modeling, and possible selection bias from using a static exhaustive table; the holdout split is used for candidate-pool separation but final regret is relative to the full table.
