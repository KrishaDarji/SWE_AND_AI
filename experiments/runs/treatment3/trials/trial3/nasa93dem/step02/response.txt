Implemented an independent NASA93DEM guess skill in source/guess_skill.py.

The CSV is validated for existence, required target, nonblank values, numeric finite effort, and nonempty rows. Effort- is the target. The model uses the remaining project attributes after excluding idX and the post-project outcome fields Defects- and MonthsX, which are potential leakage. Numeric fields are passed through; categorical fields are one-hot encoded; a RandomForestRegressor estimates effort. The reusable EffortGuesser API returns a prediction and ensemble-tree standard deviation. The CLI accepts --csv and optional feature=value pairs.

Measured run: 93 rows, 25 features, 74 training rows, 19 held-out rows, MAE 473.2256826315792, RMSE 1299.0489688158339, R2 0.4981667106966079. Tests: 3 passed. Runtime: 0.71 seconds real time.

Assumptions and uncertainty: historical NASA93DEM projects are representative of a new proposal; input categories are comparable; and a random held-out split is an acceptable estimate. The reported tree spread is model disagreement, not a calibrated prediction interval. The dataset is small and heterogeneous, so a single split can be unstable and large projects can dominate MAE/RMSE. Random splitting may also place related projects in both train and test; grouped or time-based validation would be stronger if project lineage or chronology were available. Kloc+ and other attributes may be known at proposal time only if the proposal is specific enough. Defects- and MonthsX are deliberately excluded because they may be outcomes measured after execution; including them would create data leakage. Other unrecognized proxy leakage may remain.
