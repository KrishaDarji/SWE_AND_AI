It is most useful when the held-out projects resemble the historical sample and their size/process attributes carry predictive signal. The median baseline remains a defensible choice when the sample is too sparse for stable feature relationships, when future projects come from a different process or era, or when avoiding model complexity is more important than average accuracy.

Uncertainty is material: the dataset contains only 93 projects, effort is right-skewed, repeated folds are not independent, and no confidence intervals or external validation are claimed. Potential bias comes from project-era effects, selection and survivorship, measurement conventions, omitted staffing/process variables, and the chosen model/tuning settings. This is predictive evidence, not causal evidence that any project attribute changes effort.
