Implemented the Step 10 race comparison in source/race.py and preserved all Step 9 source, tests, and demonstrations.

The race uses identical eligibility for the deterministic choose selector and a uniformly sampled historical baseline: complete finite configurations, nonmissing criterion, 0 <= N_estimators <= cap, and three finite outcomes. It preserves original CSV positions, uses fresh local random.Random(seed) instances for seeds 42–51, reports exclusions/counts, and computes baseline MRE- minus selector MRE-. Positive differences favor the selector. ACC+ and PRED40+ differences are labeled baseline-selector. The fixed tolerance is 1e-12.

The complete suite has 35 passing tests. The dataset demonstration reports 10 wins, 0 ties, 0 losses; mean MRE- difference 0.7697643593519882 and median 0.824300441826215. The successful race timing is real 0.09 seconds in runtime_output.txt. The selector sees all historical outcomes while the baseline samples one row, so this is retrospective benchmarking, not an equal-budget search or proof of future performance. Seeds are repeated selections from one dataset, not independent LLM trials; no significance or generalization claim is made. N_estimators is only a compute proxy.

Reused: the Step 9 parsing, finite-number conventions, configuration schema, choose ordering, and all prior modules/tests. Added: race eligibility, seeded baseline sampling, paired summaries, direction labels, edge-case validation, and race tests. The authorized EZR reference informed the conservative retrospective comparison and explicit tie-breaking/summary reporting; no Lua or third-party package was used.

Assumptions and limitations: CSV rows with wrong field counts are malformed; missing/nonfinite settings or outcomes are invalid; a cap is finite and nonnegative; empty/no-feasible populations return explicit statuses; a single feasible row is sampled repeatedly. Historical outcomes can be optimistic and do not measure runtime or future generalization.
