The implementation uses only Python’s standard library. It translates the authorized EZR machinery for streamed CSV consumption, one-pass numeric accumulation (Welford-style mean and m2), the trailing '-' lower-is-better convention, and defensive copying. It processes records in existing CSV order, assigns stable one-based record IDs, skips missing/nonfinite outcomes, retains the first best result on ties, and preserves prior snapshots.

Verification passed: 7 tests. The demonstration processed 1,152 records; mean 461.7535963541664, sample variance 26426.313365992526, and best record 71 at Performance- 244.23. /usr/bin/time -p reported real 0.06 seconds.

Assumptions and limitations: CSV order is a demonstration sequence, not a verified timeline. Lower Performance- is better. Empty input has no best result and no variance; one valid observation has no sample variance. The evaluator makes no claim of convergence, sufficient evidence, or an automatic stopping rule.
