Statistical procedure and rationale
The estimand is the difference in population mean raw effort, useful for planning average resource needs. Welch's independent two-sample two-sided t procedure allows unequal variances and unequal sample sizes. Alpha is fixed at 0.05, with a corresponding 95% confidence interval. The two-sided procedure can detect either a reduction or an increase; the sign determines direction. Standard error = 261.976626; Welch degrees of freedom = 15.552581; t = 0.651341; p = 0.524335. Difference confidence interval = [-386.030808, 727.303097]. This interval is compatible with lower and higher candidate mean effort. No statistically detected difference; no proof of equivalence.

Independent within-group percentile bootstrap sensitivity: 20,000 resamples, numpy default_rng seed 42, preserving sample sizes; 95% interval [-293.642982, 682.069608]. This is supplementary and does not drive a second significance decision. scikit-learn is retained for Step 2 prediction; NumPy and SciPy, its installed required dependencies, provide the statistical calculations. No model predictions are treated as observed effort.

Practical criterion
A reduction of at least 10% of the observed baseline mean (60.606386 units) is an illustrative planning threshold. Relative thresholds avoid inventing physical units, but 10% is not an externally validated economic requirement and should be replaced by a manager-approved threshold before a decision. The threshold is data-dependent and its sampling uncertainty is not propagated. An observed reduction can meet the threshold descriptively; stronger evidence requires the entire Welch difference interval below -60.606386. Neither condition holds here. Statistical significance alone would not establish practical relevance.

Manager conclusion
These historical observations do not establish that tool=h is less costly. Its observed mean effort is higher, but the uncertainty is wide and the test does not detect a population mean difference. Further evidence with appropriate project comparability is needed before claiming savings. Failure to detect a difference is not proof of equivalence.

Assumptions and limitations
Welch inference requires independent projects and approximately normal sampling distributions of the group means, with finite variances and representative sampling. The candidate group has only 10 projects; both groups show substantial dispersion and the baseline includes an extreme value. Mean-based inference and the bootstrap may be fragile under this skew and sample imbalance. The small_sample_warning field is an operational flag for n<10, not assurance of validity at n=10. Dependence among projects or centers is not modeled. Project size and other characteristics can confound raw effort differences: these were observational groups, not randomized process assignments. No causal adjustment or causal cost savings are claimed. Missing outcomes are excluded without imputation and can cause selection bias when present. No equivalence analysis is performed. This comparison does not complete the separate required same analysis, whose exact procedure was not supplied.
